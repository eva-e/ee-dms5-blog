    Stage 9 Collaboration Update - Week 12:

    'The Bird and The Branches' by Pita Khanngern:
    In Week 13, I worked on the final refinements for the Denial Stage as well as the Anger Stage. After sending Pita the new draft for Denial, she preferred the single layer track of piano rather than the new synth instrument replacing the melodic notes. She suggested that I keep the atmospheric sounds however lower the level, to not overpower the piano, as it comes across overwhelming. She also requested a distinction between these two stages, as I sent her the first draft of the Anger stage, which she found had a similar tone to the Denial stage. I believe she picked up on the same notes which were used in the Denial Stage. I used the same notes however played them in an abstract way without any melody or rhythm. I went into an abstract soundscape direction for the Anger stage, as this stage reflected instability, rage and irritation. I used the Absynth 6 instrument library for the Anger stage, layering multiple instruments for the track. Below I have attached a link to a GDrive folder with screenshots of my DAW and drafts.

    Improvements/Reflection:

    Worked on the sound effects earlier, however I dedicated a week to working on the short documentary composition due to Jolie's early deadline. The feedback I received on Thursday's Final Presentation mentioned the Anger Stage soundtrack lacking a larger distinction in comparison to the Denial stage, as it came across too subtle. In going forward with Pita's project, I plan to emphasise the intensity of this level with aggressive instruments such as dark atmos and synths on Absynth 6, Kontakt libraries and Massive X. From my experience with sound, I haven't composed intense or anger themed tracks before, however I tend to really enjoy listening to bass music, not overly bass boosted, however you feel it radiate at an appropriate level, as well as experimental synth/ambient focused compositions. For the sound effects, I took more time focusing on the soundtrack and wasn't able to fully implement them, consequently the soundtrack was a standalone composition. I recorded the foley sounds however wasn't able to mix and clean the sounds. The sounds have been included in the folder below, as part of my work during the semester.

    In addition to improving this track, I was deeply inspired and impressed by a student's composition from the MESS major project class, I believe named Yanni, who composed a long form track which included a few different instrumentation sections. His composition was one of the best I have heard by a student. His ability to layer diverse instruments with a melodic foundation was beautiful. There were instances where multiple instruments entered and cut off, returning back to a single accoustic guitar/piano melody. Here I felt compelled to bring back the short lived melodic moments. This was a strong way to draw an audience into the realm of sound, enabling them to feel attached to short lived instances of sound, which I definitely did. The section I admired the most was the synth and retro sound effects section, I also loved how he kept this constant beat transmitting from the front left speaker, while other instruments flowed in and became one large layered track. Overall outstanding and in admiration of his level of musical intellect.
