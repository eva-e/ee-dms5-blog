Week 11 Reflection:

May 18 - May 24

Peer Feedback - Task 5

Jonathan Lewin -
Collaborative Project (Music and SFX) - Jam Game (1 week - Finished) - Maulder's Farm:
The soundtrack sets the tone of the game really nicely. I love how the title screen and in-game music are separate pieces, yet compliment each other rather pleasantly. The instruments sound crisp, a little chiptune like and have a retro or minimalist feel, which pairs quite well with the low-res textured environment. I noticed that the sound effect of walking on the gravel was the same as the grass, which didn't bother me, though in comparison to other games I've played such as Minecraft, I am used to this change in surface sound. For example grass makes a rustling or sweeping like sound, compared to a grainy and rocks shuffling type sound for gravel.
There's an instance or large amount of time where the sound disappears and I found it quite appealing, as it amplified the sound effects which brought the game closer to reality and a little unsettling amongst the fast forest and open lands.

Syl Natario-Yong -
Animation Project:
This is a really beautiful and strong soundtrack, I especially love the melody of the traditional instruments. I believe that due to the abstract nature of the visuals, I cannot articulate a clear meaning, however the mysterious undertones of the soundtrack are quite fitting, resulting in an individual interpretation. The traditional instruments and reverb applied to the sound effects, align nicely with the ripple animations. The chime like instrument melody reflects something eerie, strange and evokes a sense of wonder. I did feel that the end sound was a little jarring, as it faded in quite abruptly, which is understandable as the visuals also build up quite quickly.
From 0:49, I may be wrong, though personally the synth instrument changed how I perceived the realm of the sound/visuals, it sounded very dreamcore and ethereal, which felt distant to the other previous instruments, tradional drums, chimes, strings. It strayed from the strange or mysterious tone to uncanny and surreality. At 1:00, I like how it fades out to nothing, aligning with the visual of just a white background.

Zac Preaudet-Sartori -
Secret Level Rescore and design_WIP scene 2:
The sound effects at the current stage are excellent. They set the scene in a subtle manner, which I would presume will be increased during mixing with the soundtrack. The first sound, reminds me of an old car engine start sound, however it could be amplified with bass as well as a heavier type of machinery sound possibly. The winds moving past are calming, similar to white noise, though it may sound too pleasant for this type of scene. At 0:20, when the bikes or heavy vehicle engines turn on, I noticed that when each vehicle was being shown, there were a mixture of engine revs and start up sounds, conveying vehicles that are starting up and others revving in the background. I liked how the ambient presence of the vehicle sounds were articulated here. Throughout the rescore, I also paid attention to sounds that followed into other scenes, such as the winds, which carried from scene 2, to the end. I wonder if the first scene could also incorporate wind sounds, seeing that the final scene with vehicles included it, due to the sandstorm presence in both scenes.
