Week 3 Reflection:

Wednesday, March 18

Rhythm Task

In today's week 3 class, the focus was on Triadic Interchange and constructing a chord progression through this harmonic technique. I created an array of chords during class however I began to overthink, focusing on changing one note for each chord, which lead to me naturally forming a melody, drifting off the path of this task. I then restarted this technique from home, with my piano. Using a piano instrument from 'The Gentleman' Kontakt library, I started with A♯, D♯, F and continued to shift the chords, lowering the octave at first and interchanging towards the end. At the same time I was altering the key. At times I played the same note in an octave higher or lower. I noticed while constructing the chord prgression I wasn't letting the shifts flow naturally, as the one note change per chord rule was constantly on my mind. Throughout the short progression I attempted to experiment with the tempo, sustaining chords, some moments of silence or making sudden shifts with the rhythm, sounding similar to a non melodic poem. Playing it back, the tone felt quite warm and welcoming.

The feedback I recieved from Jack was related to the appeal of the chord progression. He mentioned that it sounded pleasing and the use of rhythm and silences was really effective, as well as the bass notes added an emotional weight. I really appreciated and am thankful for this feedback, as I was pleased it conveyed an emotional weight, without a melody. It intrigued me how a simple chord progression was able to sonically express emotions, with only tonal shifts, lacking a melodic component.
