Stage 1 Collaboration Update - Week 5:

'a Living Masterpiece' by Calvin Araya:
This week Calvin and I discussed the sound design and score, specifically a variety of references and his animation progress. Currently, Calvin has completed the storyboarding, written draft and sketches for his animation. Below I have included a few notes from Calvin regarding the SFX, written draft and music pacing. This coming week, I will be watching the first draft of the animation and noting my ideation/vision for the score.

  • There are points where the music can either continue or drop down to give you a break. The animation is 3 minutes long.
  • Potential SFX: Bird flapping, death bird noises, doors shut/open, footsteps, nature, fireplace crackling, big vines growing, etc. These are all optional, it would be best to place these in after the base ost is applied to the animatic, so we can use sound to fill in spaces, rather than distract from the ost.
  • Written Draft:
    In an isolated museum filled with overgrown vines and the marks of time in a post-apocalyptic lush world, the statue David stands in the hall; a renowned hero of old. He remembers one hundred years ago when people used to walk in the glowing hall of gold, children often admiring him as the giant slayer.

    Present day, there are no children, only isolation. All the other art pieces are content staying within the 4 corners of their frame, however this hero yearns for more. He faces towards the window, and he can see the death crows which hunt after human blood. The sight of nature also stirs his soul, what a lush beautiful world.

    All of a sudden a young boy named Cody rushes into the museum with a fatal wound. He lays against the window looking towards and admiring Michelangelo's David, "the hero of old." The death crows bash against the door hunting after human blood, Cody cannot hold himself any longer, muttering "I want to live." David awakens and carries the boy in his arms, the dark crows not being able to find him in the museum.

    David places Cody next to a campfire, then finds out that Cody is a young artist. He loves music and painting. Cody Awakens, and across many years they both survive together in the open world, only for David to realise that whatever material he was sculpted from was not meant to last in outdoor conditions, the cracks and deteriorating slowly rising. -The Death crows find Cody, then in the valley between the dangerous world and Cody's freedom, David sacrifices himself and uses his soul power to entrap the dark menaces and set a vine net in the chasm. The rebound hero allowed for Cody to cross over to a safe life. Cody returns years later as a father with children, visiting the statue every year to remember who inspired him to survive and create .. Forever remembered, a living masterpiece. The End

    'a Living Masterpiece' by Calvin Araya:
    On Tuesday April 7th, Calvin, Chantelle and I met up to discuss our collaboration. Calvin's sister will be co animating and rendering some scenes, with the role of Assistant Animator and Background Artist. Calvin also completed a 3 minute draft, with the animation now shifting towards an Animation Short Film. We watched the draft and took notes accordingly. Chantelle suggested a context scene at the beginning similar to a prologue, which gives a small background about the present world of the animation. The animation draft included sound notes, such as the tone or mood of each scene, as well as the voiceover script. I also took down notes while watching.

  • Opening scene, Death Crows: Sinister, eerie, disturbing, atmospheric, strings, dramatic melody. When crows are in scene chords shift back to the opening scene melody, something sinister is about to happen.

    • Later in the day I re-watched the animation and loaded it into Ableton, drafting melodies and chord progressions. I have attached a screen recording of the first and second draft on Ableton, where I composed for the first minute and a half of the animation. After composing both, I sent them to Calvin and Chantelle. Calvin perceived the first one as more unique and memorable than the second. The second posed to be a little too dark and sinister, as Calvin has a gradual tone in mind which is ascending. Dramatic and sinister at the start, when the death crows are present, shifting to hope, adventure, back to a dramatic tone and finally a bittersweet ending tone.

    Stage 3 Collaboration Update - Week 6 :

    'a Living Masterpiece' by Calvin Araya & Chantelle Araya:
    This week I received animation updates from both Calvin and Chantelle. Calvin sent through short clips of his animation progress, while Chantelle focused on rendering some scenes. Below I have attached a screenshot of a rendered scene by Chantelle. Chantelle will be focusing on Background Rendering.
    My focus this week was on continuing ideation. A few challenges arose, which surrounded my own thought process and ability. I tend to overthink and question myself, for example, will my collaborator be satisfied with my composition and is this enough? I found it difficult to expand on my current chord progression, from the two compositions I have drafted. Speicifally, the most challenging collaboration is 'a Living Masterpiece', as it features a fluctuating tone/mood, which was suggested by Calvin, however, my indecisive and doubtful thoughts slightly caused an obstacle.

    In saying this, going forward, I plan to allocate a set amount of time for each soundtrack, organising my time in a way that allows for brainstorming and regaining focus, which will benefit my progression as well as overcoming the creative block. So far, I have a system, where I have separated each project into Google Docs pages, noting down and organising my thought process, as well as any feedback or requirements I have been given. Weekly updates and communication have been working well in all three projects.
