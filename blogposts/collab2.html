'a Living Masterpiece' by Calvin Araya:
This week Calvin and I discussed the sound design and score, specifically a variety of references and his animation progress. Currently, Calvin has completed the storyboarding, written draft and sketches for his animation. Below I have included a few notes from Calvin regarding the SFX, written draft and music pacing. This coming week, I will be watching the first draft of the animation and noting my ideation/vision for the score.

  • There are points where the music can either continue or drop down to give you a break. The animation is 3 minutes long.
  • Potential SFX: Bird flapping, death bird noises, doors shut/open, footsteps, nature, fireplace crackling, big vines growing, etc. These are all optional, it would be best to place these in after the base ost is applied to the animatic, so we can use sound to fill in spaces, rather than distract from the ost.
  • Written Draft:
    In an isolated museum filled with overgrown vines and the marks of time in a post-apocalyptic lush world, the statue David stands in the hall; a renowned hero of old. He remembers one hundred years ago when people used to walk in the glowing hall of gold, children often admiring him as the giant slayer.

    Present day, there are no children, only isolation. All the other art pieces are content staying within the 4 corners of their frame, however this hero yearns for more. He faces towards the window, and he can see the death crows which hunt after human blood. The sight of nature also stirs his soul, what a lush beautiful world.

    All of a sudden a young boy named Cody rushes into the museum with a fatal wound. He lays against the window looking towards and admiring Michelangelo's David, "the hero of old." The death crows bash against the door hunting after human blood, Cody cannot hold himself any longer, muttering "I want to live." David awakens and carries the boy in his arms, the dark crows not being able to find him in the museum.

    David places Cody next to a campfire, then finds out that Cody is a young artist. He loves music and painting. Cody Awakens, and across many years they both survive together in the open world, only for David to realise that whatever material he was sculpted from was not meant to last in outdoor conditions, the cracks and deteriorating slowly rising. -The Death crows find Cody, then in the valley between the dangerous world and Cody's freedom, David sacrifices himself and uses his soul power to entrap the dark menaces and set a vine net in the chasm. The rebound hero allowed for Cody to cross over to a safe life. Cody returns years later as a father with children, visiting the statue every year to remember who inspired him to survive and create .. Forever remembered, a living masterpiece. The End

    Stage 4 Collaboration Update - Week 7:

    'a Living Masterpiece' by Calvin Araya & Chantelle Araya:
    As of this week the soundtrack has taken a turn. It didn’t completely appeal to me, it felt like it was lacking something. I wanted to provide Calvin and Chantelle with a melody and tone that contrasts with the current draft. Last week I had a creative block which hindered my progression. On Monday, after hours of sitting at the piano, ideating and improvising, I finally drafted a new melody with undertones of warmth and slight heartache, in comparison to the bright and epic track I had first composed. It was the moment when I placed myself in the scene and embodied the emotions of each character, that resulted in the new track. I noticed the first track lacked poetic connotations. In the new draft, I further leaned into dynamic notes, variation and poetic elements. Both Calvin and Chantelle have been updating me weekly, sometimes twice a week with their animation updates as well as notifying me if they’re busy. I have also been updating them on sound drafts. There are several tonal shifts in this animatic, especially the fluctuation of emotions throughout it. My plan is to continue layering and refining the soundtrack, while following the updates of the animation.
    Both Calvin and Chantelle were very pleased with the new track. Calvin mentioned it reminded him of a Zelda Breath of The Wild soundtrack.
    I also recieved an updated version of the animation at the end of Week 7. The animation is now 4 minutes long, as Calvin slowed the pace, as well as added a few scenes. I will use the updated version for composition developments.

    • Stage 10 Collaboration Update - Week 13:

    'a Living Masterpiece' by Calvin & Chantelle Araya: For the final three days of our collaboration, Calvin and Chantelle worked on completing the animation and rendering. They were both updating me frequently regarding where they were up to. While they were working on the animation, I was refining the soundtrack, re-recording my previously composed notes and adding in more sound effects from Pixabay. I was planning to use the Splice library however due to the amount of time that was left and the price of Splice's subscription, I decided to find quality sounds from Pixabay, as well as cleaning them through RX, EQ and Reverb if needed.
    Calvin sent me the final animation video at around an hour before submission. I added the final video to my DAW and aligned my sound tracks to the new pacing with the full animation frames now included.

    As mentioned last week, Calvin and Chantelle were planning to voice the characters in the animation, however did not have an ample amount of time to record due to their other commitments. Personally, I did not have a problem if the voiceover wasn't included, though it would have been a strong element for the animation, giving a foundational layer in terms of context for the visuals. In saying this, around thirty minutes prior to submission, Calvin suddenly decided to include the voiceover. He mentioned that he had just recorded voiceovers for the script and phrases himself. I was hesitant and unsure whether I would be able to implement them into the track, knowing that it also takes time to export and upload to the GDrive folder. With the tension growing and thirty minutes remaining, I decided to include the voiceovers, as I felt that it grounds the story. While some animations avoid voiceovers, understandably it isn't always necessary, this animation was heavily centred on a deep story. The addition of subtle phrases was enough to convey its profound themes.

    Improvements/Reflection:

    Similar to my other collaborations, this project went quite well. The prompt communication, updates, feedback and attentive focus on our project development, made this collaboration experience enjoyable and less stressful, even though it was an overwhelming amount of work. I appreciate that my collaborators were attentive, especially to my needs or deadline. I was also certainly taking into consideration their time, efforts and other commitments.
    I was disappointed that I could not fully mix the voiceover tracks in time. While the tracks did not sound out of place in terms of alignment with the subtitles, they clearly did not fade in or out smoothly, consisting of abrupt cuts. The second last line, "Go..Live..", had a keyboard spacebar sound, which was bothering to hear while I was editing, knowing that I could not fix it in time. From my perspective, I found the orchestral entrance at the end underwhelming. I asked Calvin about this and he perceived it as a good volume. Something about it was not empowering enough to me. Overall, while I am undoubtedly happy, I feel that I could have composed with more complexity or dynamic orchestral elements, however the way that it turned out met the expectations of my collaborators, which is all I hoped for.
