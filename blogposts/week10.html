Week 10 Reflection:

May 11 - May 17

Composer Research Task - Joe Hisaishi

I created a fictional phrase for Nissan, "More style, less luxury". I intended it to be a jingle, as usually jingles have catch phrases, including Nestle, Burger King etc. I noticed that these brands don't mention their name, however use slogans or catch phrases, making it more memorable alongside a melodic tune. I believe Nissan was the closest to a jingle, compared to the other two, as I was not able to generate a shorter piece for Zara or Arnott's. The prompt I used for the Nissan jingle was: "Generate a 10 second short ident for Nissan motor corp. Something with a deep house beat, a lot of bass. Slow paced. A clean and polished sound. Also add some ethereal sounds. Lyrics: More style, less luxury".

It became more jingle like than an ident, as it went for about 20 seconds and I added lyrics, which is my mistake. It was difficult for the AI to generate short audio.

The difference between a jingle and ident that I discovered was that jingles tend to create an association with a product, sometimes directly or indirectly. For example, Old Spice, their jingle is a short whistle tune, with a few notes. The name isn't mentioned, however the array of notes make it memorable alongside the whistling. Other brands, like Burger King, use a short lyrical song which mentions the food on their menu. On the other hand, idents, don't include phrases however serve as the sonic identity, a 5 second sound ranging from a buzz, vibration, strings, twinkles, chimes etc. Idents remind me of sound effects, aligning with the brand logo or animation. The Disney+ start up sound is one that is quite appealing to me. I notice how it uses orchestral chords, followed by a small click sound. It follows the movement of the animation. When the plus symbol emits a blue glow, the strings pitch increases and a click sound follows once the plus symbol returns to white.

I anticipated for the Zara ident to be short and reflect the Zara clothing style, as well as their brand overall, however Suno generated a generic sounding song. My prompt was:"Generate a only 10 second short ident for Zara. A clothing brand, slow paced like runway music. Fashion show aesthetic. House type beat, not upbeat, more serious walking type." While I did add that it should not be upbeat, leaning towards a serious piece, it generated a stereotypical 'runway' song.

My final track for Arnott's, which I intended to be a jingle, resulted in an overly playful song. My prompt was: "Generate a 30 second jingle for Arnott's biscuits, with warm and bright undertones, Include some crunch sounds to reflect eating biscuits, The catch phrase is "Just the snack I need". Suno generated a decent length track, however, it did not include the crunch sound or slogan that I added in my prompt. The warm and bright undertones were taken as playful by Suno. While it did attempt to compose a bright and upbeat track, it lacked a unique tone.

Overall, my stance in regards to using AI for music creation is to avoid it. I understand the benefit of convenience and efficiency for deadlines or limited time limits, however this defeats our purpose as creatives. The beauty of being a creative is the process, the journey and inspiration that led to the finished product. Our personal experiences and interests are the foundation of our sound and music composition. AI takes the poetry out of ideation, it limits our ability to profoundly contemplate. I find the generated songs shallow and lacking a heart. It's undeniable that the industry relies on it and I wouldn't say I am against it being used as a tool, more so, I oppose it's dominance over our thinking process.
